`research_workspace/06_codebooks/affective_intermediation_coding_codebook_v1.md`

## Core coding domains

### Emotion intensity (0–3)
Fear, anxiety/uncertainty, anger, moral outrage, grief/sadness, solidarity, care/compassion, empathy, hope, gratitude, pride, reassurance, defiance, urgency, and other emotion.

### Situational appraisal
Threat, harm/loss, injustice, uncertainty, responsibility/blame, intentionality, norm violation, vulnerability, controllability, coping efficacy, collective efficacy, care need, and opportunity/hope.

### Communication function
Inform, warn, reassure, regulate fear, mobilize, advocate, provide service, fundraise, build solidarity, generate empathy, moral evaluation, increase efficacy, mourn/commemorate, document/testify, celebrate, encourage defiance, and other.

### Action orientation
Whether the item asks, enables, or encourages action; action type; specificity; and immediacy.

### Public uptake
Validation, gratitude, practical inquiry, help request, help offer, fear, grief, anger, solidarity, hope, contestation, resource sharing, coordination, and calls to action.

### Affective circulation
Official share/repost counts where visible, minimum observed recirculation traces, tagging, organizational tagging, and cross-platform movement.

### Affective conversion
Observable movement toward help seeking/offering, donation, volunteering, attendance, protest participation, service connection, resource referral, reporting/documentation, or other action.

## Core distinction

**Engagement volume is not emotion intensity.**

Reactions, comments, shares, reposts, and views indicate relational uptake/circulation. Emotional meaning is coded independently from the communication or interaction itself.

## Reliability

A 40-item stratified reliability pilot precedes full-corpus coding. Coding definitions should be revised only before final codebook freeze and before substantive hypothesis testing.
